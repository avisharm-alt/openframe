# Partner onboarding

A **partner** is an organisation that serves people directly (a shelter, an outreach team, a drop-in) and wants to receive specific items. Partners post requests; student teams deliver to the partner's site; the partner's staff hand items to the person. This page is for the chapter coordinator who verifies a partner.

Partners and workers are **verified by a coordinator of the partner's chapter** before anything can be posted. Nobody else can approve them.

## 1. Before you add anyone

1. Meet or call the organisation. Explain the three rules: requests are about **items, never people**; students **never deal with recipients** (we deliver to staff at the delivery site); and OpenFrame **never records who receives what**.
2. Agree the **delivery site(s)** (a public name, address and receiving hours) and any **items they cannot accept** (for example "used underwear, glass items").
3. Agree who at the organisation may post. Usually two or three frontline workers or a team lead.
4. Have the organisation sign the one-page **partnership note** below (or keep an email thread that says the same). Keep it with your chapter records, not in OpenFrame.

*The note is a proposal. It is not legal advice and has not been reviewed by a lawyer; the owner should confirm the wording before a partner signs it (see [SAFETY-OPERATIONS.md](SAFETY-OPERATIONS.md)).*

## 2. Add the partner

Two ways:

- **You add it.** Coordinate → **Partners** → *New partner* (name, public description, items they do not accept). It is verified straight away. Then add its **delivery sites** under the partner.
- **They apply.** Someone signs in and uses *Is your organisation not listed?* on **For partners**. The application appears on Coordinate → **Approvals** as unverified, and cannot be used until you click **Verify partner**. Reject it if you cannot verify them.

## 3. Approve its workers

Each worker signs in with Google, opens **For partners**, and asks for access to the partner. Their request appears on Coordinate → **Approvals**. Approve only people the organisation named. A pending worker cannot post or see anything. Remove a worker from the Partners tab when they leave the organisation.

## 4. What a good request looks like

- Item, size, how many, when it is needed: "Men's winter boots, size 11, 6 pairs, by Friday, deliver to Ark Aid main building".
- The short public note (140 characters) is for the item only: "wide fit if possible". The form refuses obvious names, phone numbers and similar, but staff are responsible for the rest.
- Urgent only when waiting would cause real harm.
- Staff **confirm receipt** in the app once the delivery arrives, which closes the loop.

See also the in-app [Guidelines](../src/app/guidelines/page.tsx) for workers.

## 5. If something goes wrong

If a request includes anything identifying, cancel it, note it in the concern queue, and talk to the partner. Suspending a partner (Partners tab) stops new requests but keeps the history. Coordinators can also confirm receipt on a worker's behalf if the worker cannot.

## Partnership note (template, one page)

> **Partnership note: [Partner organisation] and [Chapter, e.g. OpenFrame London (Western)]**
>
> Date: ________  Chapter coordinator: ________  Partner contact: ________ (role: ______)
>
> **What this is.** [Partner organisation] ("the partner") will use [the platform] to ask for specific items for the people it serves. A student-run chapter ("the chapter") will source those items from neighbours and its own small stock and deliver them to the partner's site. This note records how we will work together. It is not a contract and either side may end it at any time by telling the other.
>
> **1. Who may post.** Only the people the partner names to the chapter, who sign in with their own account and are approved by a chapter coordinator: __________________. The partner will tell the chapter when someone leaves.
>
> **2. Delivery sites.** The chapter will deliver only to these sites, during their receiving hours: ______________________________ (name, address, hours).
>
> **3. What we will not accept or request.** The partner does not accept: ______________________________. The partner will not request medication, anything needing refrigeration, or items it cannot hand out safely. The chapter does not source items outside the shared catalog.
>
> **4. No recipient information.** Requests describe items, never people. The partner's staff will not write names, descriptions, health details, locations or stories about individuals in a request or note. The chapter does not ask for, record or keep any information about the people who receive items. The only record is the partner, the delivery site, the item and the dates.
>
> **5. Students do not deal with recipients.** The chapter's volunteers hand items to the partner's staff at the delivery site and leave. Staff hand them to the person. Volunteers will not enter private areas, meet clients, or take photographs.
>
> **6. Confirming delivery.** Partner staff will confirm receipt in the app (or tell the coordinator) so the chapter knows the item arrived. The chapter aims to deliver within 72 hours of a request but cannot promise it.
>
> **7. Condition of items.** Items are new unless a request says clean second-hand is fine (for example winter coats and boots). Partner staff may decline anything they judge unsuitable.
>
> **8. Independence.** The partner and the chapter are independent. Neither speaks for the other, and listing the partner on the site is not an endorsement. The chapter is student-run, is not affiliated with any university and does not provide insurance or services.
>
> **9. Concerns.** Either side can raise a concern with the other's contact above at any time.
>
> Signed for the partner: ________________  Signed for the chapter: ________________
